## Idea review — SurvTD: Duration-Discounted Temporal-Difference Consistency for Dynamic Survival Analysis under Irregular Observation

**Decomposition**

- **Problem / gap.** Two named claims. (i) "Temporal-consistency survival methods (TCSR, DeepTCSR) assume an exogenous uniform unit step Delta t = 1, so the non-event signal carried by an interval is one bit regardless of how much time elapsed"; (ii) their backward update "renormalises the next-step lifetime distribution by dividing by the interval survival probability, which diverges exactly in the high-risk regime where S approaches zero."
- **Method (the move).** Replace the division by a composition: a renewal rightward shift $\Phi_{+\Delta t}$ (from $R_j = R_{j+1} + \Delta t_j$) followed by the triangular categorical projection $\Pi$ onto a fixed remaining-lifetime grid, with an interval discount $\gamma_j = S_{\theta^-}(\Delta t_j)$ read off a frozen EMA target network; multi-step targets mixed with $\lambda_j = \lambda^{\Delta t_j/\delta_s}$; fit by Cramér distance; censored tails completed by IPCW.
- **Why it should work (as argued).** "$\Pi \Phi$ is non-expansive in the Cramer (L2-on-CDF) metric and conserves total mass exactly"; the target's "mass sums to unity by construction"; "With theta- held frozen the map is affine in p and its Cramer-metric modulus is bounded by gamma_j, which is strictly below unity whenever the per-bin hazard is bounded away from zero"; and the duration-geometric $\lambda$ makes "the effective bootstrapping horizon ... invariant to sampling rate."
- **Assumptions inferred (not stated by the idea).** (a) $\Delta t_j$ is bounded away from $0$, or $S_{\theta^-}$ carries an off-grid interpolation convention; (b) censoring is independent of covariates *and* of the event time, $C \perp (T,X)$, for the marginal Kaplan–Meier $\hat G$ to give unbiased IPCW weights; (c) the one-step target is the *mixture* form, not the realized-indicator form written in Step 5 (see below); (d) the Cramér loss is squared, not the bare metric; (e) the terminal bin carries "$\ge s_K$" semantics rather than "$= s_K$".

I checked each mathematical assertion rather than reading past it. What follows separates the ones that are correct from the ones that are not, with the counterexample in each failing case. Numerical checks below were run on a $K=64$, $\delta_s=1$ grid.

---

### What is correct

These are real and I want them on the record before the objections, because several are things this literature routinely gets wrong.

1. **The metric is named correctly, and it is the right metric.** "Cramer (L2-on-CDF)" is the correct identification of $\ell_2(F,G) = (\int (F-G)^2\,dx)^{1/2}$. Choosing Cramér rather than Wasserstein is not cosmetic: the categorical projection $\Pi$ is the $\ell_2$-orthogonal projection onto grid-supported CDFs and is therefore non-expansive in Cramér, while it is *not* non-expansive in Wasserstein (Rowland et al. 2018 give the counterexample). A write-up that claimed "Wasserstein" here would be wrong; this one is not.

2. **$\Pi\Phi$ is non-expansive in Cramér — verified, including the horizon clip.** $\Phi_{+\Delta t}$ acts on the CDF as $F \mapsto F(\cdot - \Delta t)$, a translation, hence an $\ell_2$ isometry; $\Pi$ is an orthogonal projection onto a convex set, hence non-expansive; clipping at $s_K$ replaces $|F_1 - F_2|$ with $0$ above $s_K$ and so strictly decreases $\ell_2$. Empirically the worst ratio $\ell_2(\Pi\Phi p, \Pi\Phi q)/\ell_2(p,q)$ over 4000 random pairs and random $\Delta t \in [0,6]$ was $0.99999$. **Claim holds.**

3. **Mass conservation holds at the horizon boundary and in the event branch.** With the stated "accumulating mass carried past the horizon $s_K$ into the terminal absorbing bin" convention (i.e. clip-then-project, as in C51), total mass is exactly $1$ for every $\Delta t$ including sub-bin, super-horizon, and $\Delta t = 0$ — verified to $10^{-12}$. The event branch is a Dirac, mass $1$; the branches are selected by a realized indicator rather than mixed, so no cross-branch bookkeeping is needed. **Claim holds.** (Two nits: the displayed kernel $(\Pi\Phi p)_k = \sum_m p_m\max(0, 1-|(s_m+\Delta t)-s_k|/\delta_s)$ *without* the clip loses mass whenever $s_m + \Delta t > s_K$, so the prose is doing work the formula does not; and $\delta_{\tau_j - t_j}$ is generally off-grid and needs $\Pi$ applied to it too. Both editorial.)

4. **The modulus really is $\gamma$, not $\sqrt{\gamma}$ — and that is a non-obvious point in the idea's favour.** A C51-trained reader will object that categorical distributional TD contracts at $\sqrt{\gamma}$ in Cramér, not $\gamma$. The reason it is $\gamma$ here is that the discount enters as a *mixture weight* over an unscaled support, whereas in C51 it *scales the support* and $\ell_2$ is homogeneous of degree $1/2$ under scaling. Numerically, with $\gamma$ frozen at $0.70$ the worst modulus over 5000 random pairs was exactly $0.7000$. The idea has the right answer available and never states the argument; a reviewer from the cited lineage will read the claim as an error.

5. **The $\lambda$ recursion reduces correctly at every endpoint.** $\lambda_j = \lambda^{\Delta t_j/\delta_s}$ gives: $\Delta t_j = \delta_s \Rightarrow \lambda_j = \lambda$ (recovers TD($\lambda$)); $\lambda = 1 \Rightarrow \lambda_j = 1$ (Monte Carlo at any sampling rate); $\lambda = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda_j = 0$ for $\Delta t_j > 0$ (one-step TD); $\Delta t_j = 0 \Rightarrow \lambda_j = 1$, i.e. co-timed observations are transparent, which is the correct behaviour. And the *weights* are genuinely subdivision-invariant: $\prod_j \lambda_j = \lambda^{\sum_j \Delta t_j/\delta_s} = \lambda^{T/\delta_s}$ depends only on total elapsed time $T$, not on how it was cut up. **This is the cleanest correct construction in the proposal.** (It is not, however, *stated* as a recursion — "$G_j = T p_j$ evaluated with mixing weight $\lambda_j$" contains no reference to $G_{j+1}$, so the object being mixed is never written down.)

6. **Cramér is also the right loss for this target.** For a squared-Cramér objective the minimiser against a randomly drawn target is the pointwise-mean CDF, so regressing onto realized Diracs $\delta_{\tau_j - t_j}$ recovers the true conditional lifetime CDF, and a mixture $\lambda$-return is consistently recovered as well. Cramér also admits unbiased sample gradients, unlike Wasserstein. The idea makes this choice and does not argue for it.

### What does not hold

**(i) The load-bearing variable $\gamma_j$ does not appear in the target equation it is claimed to be the modulus of.**

Step 5 writes

$$T p_j = \mathbb{1}[\text{event}]\cdot \delta_{\tau_j - t_j} + (1 - \mathbb{1}[\text{event}])\cdot (\Pi\Phi_{+\Delta t_j} p_{\theta^-})_j .$$

There is no $\gamma_j$ anywhere in it. The indicator is a *realized observation*, so conditional on the transition the map is either constant (event branch, modulus $0$) or $\Pi\Phi$ (no-event branch, modulus $\le 1$, non-expansive but **not** a contraction). The sentence "its Cramer-metric modulus is bounded by gamma_j" therefore does not follow from the displayed operator. The intended operator is almost certainly the renewal *mixture*

$$\mathcal{T}\eta_j = (1-\gamma_j)\,\mu_{[0,\Delta t_j)} + \gamma_j\,\Pi\Phi_{+\Delta t_j}\eta_{j+1},$$

for which the modulus is exactly $\gamma_j$ (item 4 above). These are different operators with different fixed points and different data requirements — the mixture form needs a normalised within-interval event-time component $\mu_{[0,\Delta t_j)}$, which cannot be formed from a single realized event and is never specified. As written, the idea's headline variable is decoration in its own equation.

**(ii) With $\gamma$ endogenous, the operator is not a contraction — it is not even non-expansive.** The idea's differentiation from `Bradtke1994` makes endogeneity the selling point: "SurvTD discounts a full lifetime distribution by an endogenous, model-predicted interval survival probability." But $\gamma(\eta) = 1 - F_\eta(\Delta t)$ is a functional of $\eta$, so

$$\mathcal{T}\eta_1 - \mathcal{T}\eta_2 = \gamma_1 \Pi\Phi(\eta_1-\eta_2) + (\gamma_1-\gamma_2)(\Pi\Phi \eta_2 - \mu),$$

and the second term is not controlled by the Cramér metric in any useful way. On the grid, $|F_k| \le \delta_s^{-1/2}\|F\|_2$, and $\ell_2(\Pi\Phi\eta_2,\mu) \le \sqrt{K\delta_s}$, giving a bound of $\gamma_1 + \sqrt{K}$ — useless for any realistic $K$. This is not a loose bound. Measured: worst modulus $2.44$ over 20 000 random pairs, and on an adversarial pair (two atoms one bin apart, mixed into uniform at strength $\varepsilon$) the modulus is $4.68$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-1}$, $5.01$ at $10^{-2}$, $5.04$ at $10^{-3}$ — it does **not** decay toward $1$ as the pair converges, so it is not a boundary artifact and there is no local non-expansiveness either. The reason is structural: $\eta \mapsto F_\eta(\Delta t)$ is a point evaluation, and two CDFs can be arbitrarily close in $\ell_2$ while differing by a constant at a fixed point (take atoms at $x_0$ and $x_0+w$ and let $w \to 0$: $\|F_1-F_2\|_2^2 = \varepsilon^2 w \to 0$ with $|F_1(x_0)-F_2(x_0)| = \varepsilon$ fixed).

The idea does hedge — "the claim made is per-iteration contraction under a frozen target, not convergence of the joint two-timescale scheme" — and I credit that as honest in intent. But the hedge must be read carefully. Freezing $\theta^-$ freezes $\gamma$ *and* freezes $p_{\theta^-}$, at which point $Tp_j$ is a constant and "affine in $p$ with modulus $\gamma_j$" is vacuous. The non-vacuous reading — $\gamma$ frozen as an exogenous constant while $\eta$ varies, so the inner loop is a $\gamma$-contraction with a unique fixed point — is correct (verified, item 4), but it is not what Step 5 says, and it leaves the outer self-consistent-field iteration with no argument at all. The computation above shows why that gap cannot be closed by a naive Cramér argument. **This is a guarantee-shaped sentence whose object shifts between the two readings.**

**(iii) The hazard condition is stated in the wrong place and is not sufficient.** "strictly below unity whenever the per-bin hazard is bounded away from zero." With $h_k \ge h_{\min} > 0$ and $\Delta t_j \ge \delta_s$, indeed $\gamma_j \le 1-h_{\min} < 1$. But $S_{\theta^-}$ is defined by Step 2 on bin boundaries only, and the idea never gives an off-grid convention. For $\Delta t_j < \delta_s$ — the ordinary case in ICU telemetry, where labs arrive minutes apart and bins are days — the piecewise-constant reading gives $\gamma_j = 1$ *exactly* and the modulus is $1$; the linear-hazard reading gives $\gamma_j = 1 - h_0 \Delta t_j/\delta_s \to 1$ as $\Delta t_j \to 0$. So the needed condition is a conjunction: **hazard bounded below AND $\Delta t_j \ge \Delta t_{\min} > 0$**, plus a stated interpolation rule for $S$ off the grid. Only the first conjunct is named. Note the shape of this: the contraction degrades to modulus $1$ precisely in the short-interval regime, and short intervals are half of what "irregular observation" means. The condition is satisfiable (impose a minimum interval, or set $\delta_s$ below the shortest gap), but each fix has a cost the idea has not priced — and shrinking $\delta_s$ makes finding (v) worse.

**(iv) The horizon bin is a strict sink, and the bias is unidirectional.** Because time only moves forward, $\Phi$ is always a rightward shift, so clipped mass at $s_K$ can only accumulate — unlike C51, where rewards of either sign push mass both ways. Simulated: an atom at $s_{20}$ carried backwards by twelve shifts of $4\delta_s$ reaches $\Pr[\text{top bin}] = 1.0$ by the eleventh, and the fixed point of the no-event recursion on a long censored trajectory is $\delta_{s_K}$ — the model is trained to assert that remaining lifetime is *exactly* $s_K$. Non-expansiveness is preserved (clipping shrinks $\ell_2$), so this is not a stability bug; it is a systematic downward bias on exactly the long-lifetime, low-risk subjects. The stated mitigation — "K and delta_s are set from the cohort follow-up quantile" — helps and I credit it, but a *quantile* leaves the upper bins thinly supported rather than unsupported, and the terminal bin's semantics ("$= s_K$" vs "$\ge s_K$", i.e. a defective mass) are never declared. Calling it "absorbing" borrows MDP vocabulary for something that is not a Markov absorbing state.

**(v) The sampling-rate-invariance claim is false at the distributional level, and the projection is why.** The idea's headline benefit is "making the effective bootstrapping horizon invariant to sampling rate." The $\lambda$ *weights* are invariant (item 5), but the number of times $\Pi$ is applied is not, and each projection of an off-grid atom adds up to $\delta_s^2/4$ of variance ($\delta_s^2/6$ on average over a uniform fractional offset). Over one identical physical duration $T = 32\delta_s$, cut into $n$ irregular pieces: $n=1 \Rightarrow \mathrm{sd}=0$; $n=8 \Rightarrow 1.15$ bins; $n=32 \Rightarrow 2.28$; $n=128 \Rightarrow 4.09$, growing as $\sqrt{n}$ and matching the $n\delta_s^2/6$ prediction. **Two subjects with identical latent risk who are observed at different frequencies receive targets of different dispersion over the same elapsed time.** The mean is preserved (verified: exactly $40.0$ at every $n$ until the clip bites), so the damage is to calibration and sharpness, not discrimination — which matters because integrated Brier score is one of the two declared outcomes and the idea predicts "calibration degrades" only in the *ablated* arm. The $\lambda$ construction fixes the horizon; nothing fixes the diffusion, and Step 7's backward recursion explicitly composes shifts, so it compounds.

**(vi) IPCW: the required assumption is unnamed, is false in the target domains, and the estimator is not a named one.** Step 6 promises "an unbiased target rather than a truncated one" from "a Kaplan-Meier estimate of the censoring distribution." A *marginal* KM $\hat G(t)$ yields unbiased IPCW weights only under completely independent censoring, $C \perp (T, X)$. Under covariate-dependent censoring you need a conditional $\hat G(t \mid X)$, and covariate-dependent censoring is the norm in every cohort named: in MIMIC-IV Sepsis-3 discharge is administrative censoring correlated with recovery; in PBC transplant and loss to follow-up track disease severity. (C-MAPSS is the opposite problem — units are run to failure, so there is essentially no censoring and the whole IPCW branch is inert on that benchmark, which means the component is untested on the benchmark where the headline concordance number lives.) Separately, "complete the target tail *with the target network prediction* reweighted by IPCW" composes two different corrections: substituting a model prediction for an unobserved tail is model-based imputation (an AIPW-style augmentation), and multiplying it by $1/\hat G$ is inverse weighting. Applying both without the doubly-robust cross-term double-corrects. The write-up does not say what quantity is unbiasedly estimated, what the weight multiplies, or whether the weight is $1/\hat G(\tilde T_j-)$ or a per-bin $1/\hat G(s_k)$. **Unbiasedness is asserted for an estimator that is not specified enough to check.**

**(vii) $\gamma_j$ predicts a quantity that is directly observed wherever it is applied.** On any interval $(t_j, t_{j+1}]$ ending in an observation of the subject alive, "did the subject survive the interval" is *data*, equal to $1$. Substituting the model's $S_{\theta^-}(\Delta t_j) < 1$ injects the model's own uncertainty as if it were evidence about an event already witnessed. The one interval where survival is genuinely unobserved is the final one to censoring — and that is Step 6's job, not $\gamma$'s. There is a legitimate defence available (Rao–Blackwellising the realized indicator against the model, expected-SARSA style, to reduce target variance), but it is not made, and it is exactly what makes the operator endogenous and therefore non-contractive by (ii). The idea cannot have both the endogeneity-as-novelty claim against `Bradtke1994` and the contraction claim; they are the same coefficient.

**(viii) Squared vs unsquared.** Step 8 says "Minimise the Cramer distance." For the mixture $\lambda$-return of Step 7 to be recovered as the minimiser, and for the realized-Dirac regression to recover the true conditional CDF, the objective must be $\ell_2^2$, not $\ell_2$ — the minimiser of the unsquared metric against a random target is a Cramér median, and the semi-gradient is then biased for the intended mixture. One character, but load-bearing.

**Naive-baseline audit: branch 1 — the naive version relies on a false premise, and confronting it is the contribution.** I constructed the naive version independently and had to construct it twice. The tempting one — *keep the unit-step consistency loss and feed $\Delta t$ to the encoder as a feature* — is what a competent practitioner would try first, and it rests on a false premise: an encoder feature cannot repair a wrong *target*. The $\div S(1)$ renormalisation is applied identically at every interval no matter what the encoder sees, so the fixed point is wrong by construction. The other naive move — drop the consistency term and use Dynamic-DeepHit — forfeits the inter-visit relation that is the lineage's entire reason to exist. So confronting the premise is the contribution, and branch 1 is the honest classification: **the hard floor does not fire.** Two caveats I will not soften. First, the confrontation is a borrowed tool (§2.6): the projected categorical Bellman operator is textbook distributional RL, and the domain-specific structure that makes this instance unsolved — censoring, and translation-rather-than-scaling — is real but argued in one clause each. Second, a *trimmings-free* variant (shift-and-project with the realized indicator, no endogenous $\gamma$, no IPCW, no $\lambda$) already closes both stated gaps, and by finding (i) that variant is *literally identical to the candidate's own Step 5*. That is not enough to make the audit return "naive suffices" — the trimmings-free variant embodies the candidate's central move, so using it as a baseline would be circular — but it is the reason $\gamma_j$'s status must be resolved before anything else.

**Novel-but-empty detector: passes.** The idea states what it predicts (AUC/IBS return to the unit-step baseline under NC-A, unchanged on a regular cohort, concordance floor 0.8791), what it changes, and what would count against it.

**Strongest point:** The operator bookkeeping is *correctly typed* in a way this literature usually gets wrong — Cramér rather than Wasserstein is the metric in which a categorical projection is non-expansive; the renewal shift is a translation and hence an isometry, so the modulus is $\gamma$ and not the $\sqrt{\gamma}$ of C51; and the horizon clip is itself non-expansive. I verified all three and they hold.

**Most fixable weakness:** Write the one-step target in the renewal *mixture* form $\mathcal{T}\eta_j = (1-\gamma_j)\mu_{[0,\Delta t_j)} + \gamma_j\,\Pi\Phi_{+\Delta t_j}\eta_{j+1}$ with $\gamma_j$ declared exogenous within the frozen-target inner loop — this single edit makes the modulus claim true (verified: exactly $0.70$ at $\gamma=0.70$), puts the load-bearing variable back into its own equation, and makes NC-A executable, raising B-soundness and D together.

### Two-layer verdict

**Hard floor: not triggered.** Checked against each of the four floor conditions. (1) No prior work matches on all four scoop axes — the scoop report's overlap level 2/5 and its explicit "No contemporaneous scoop found" stand, and nothing in my own analysis contradicts it (though I note its caveat that OpenAlex/S2/DBLP/Crossref were unreachable, so published-venue recall is thin). (2) The naive-baseline audit returns **branch 1**, not "naive suffices" — see the audit above, including why the trimmings-free variant does not count as the naive baseline. (3) The anti-pattern composition (`assumption_audit_and_pivot` → `architectural_operator_substitution`) has its required mitigation available and insertable: the mixture form. (4) D does not collapse — NC-B and NC-C are executable as written and the prediction has a losing branch, even though NC-A needs repair first.

**Soft judgment: `revise`.** Not `advance`: the borderline is not trivial, because three separate mathematical assertions in `core_mechanism_steps` do not follow as written, and one of them concerns the declared load-bearing variable — an experiment run against the current write-up would ablate a coefficient that the stated target does not contain. Not `abandon`: the corrected operator is a genuine and correctly-typed contribution, every failing item has a named repair, and the two findings that require experiments (projection diffusion, horizon sink) are measurable within the stated compute budget.
