# Phase 1 — Bottleneck Identification & Method-Lineage Tree

## 1. Structural Bottleneck Statement

> Prior temporal consistency survival frameworks (`Maystre2022`, `DeepTCSR2024`) enforce step-by-step risk consistency by assuming an **exogenous, stationary Markov transition with uniform unit intervals $\Delta t = 1$**, where surviving a single step yields only a 1-bit non-event signal and updating downstream hazard requires a numerically explosive $\div S(\Delta t)$ division. 
> In critical care and non-linear degradation systems, observation timestamps are fundamentally generated by an **Informative Observation Process** (`Lin2001`, `Alaa2017`) where clinical sampling frequency directly reflects latent patient deterioration ($\lambda_{\text{obs}}(t) \propto \text{Risk}(t)$), breaking the exogenous stationary transition assumption. 
> Because existing temporal-difference methods cannot scale cumulative non-event survival evidence across multi-bucket continuous intervals $\Delta t$ and suffer from $\div S$ divergence, deep survival analysis remains trapped in a false dichotomy: either falling back to high-variance terminal Monte Carlo NLL (`Lee2019`, `Bleistein2024`) which ignores inter-step consistency, or enforcing discrete uniform consistency that collapses under clinically realistic informative irregular sampling.

### Gap Classification:
- **Subtractive Gap (at shared ancestor of TCSR)**:
  Every temporal-consistency survival method inherits the assumption that observation intervals are exogenous, stationary, and uniform ($\Delta t = 1$). Removing this assumption allows the model to treat $\Delta t$ as an endogenous informative carrier of multi-bucket survival evidence.
- **Additive Gap (at leaf)**:
  A continuous-time Semi-Markov Bellman Operator with Cramér-distance contraction mapping that scales survival credit proportionally to continuous duration $\Delta t$, replacing $\div S$ explosion with contractive multiplication $\times S(\Delta t)$ and multi-step $\lambda$-return unification.
